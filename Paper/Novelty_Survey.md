# ThermoWatch — prior art and novelty assessment

**Verdict (23 November 2026):** A potentially distinct empirical contribution, **not** a new placement algorithm or a first-of-its-kind CFD workflow. In the sources checked, we did not verify an earlier study that crossed the same high-load rack placements with individual cooling-coil losses at retained flow and explicitly compared the worst-case placement decisions of a reduced model and 3D CFD. Prior art already covers the constituent ideas, including directly checking placement ranks against CFD. The closest unresolved publication is Zhang et al. (2015). The nominal CFD lead of B05 is not numerically resolved.

## Screening criteria

To challenge the contribution, look for prior work that combines (1) a rack or workload-placement decision, (2) multiple cooling-loss scenarios, (3) independent higher-fidelity evaluation on the same candidates and scenarios, and (4) an explicit comparison of the decisions or rankings, not just temperatures. A paper can be relevant without matching all four; using a different solver or GUI is not by itself a research distinction. Record whether each comparison is supported by full text, an author abstract, or title and metadata only. A targeted survey does not prove that no precedent exists.

## What the current benchmark actually establishes

ThermoWatch uses an explicit 2D heat balance to evaluate 32 placements for one high-load rack. The frozen OpenFOAM comparison covers **three shortlisted slots, one hall, and three conditions**: full cooling, CRAC-1 coil off, and CRAC-3 coil off. The fans keep operating during the two outages. The benchmark matches 52,128 rack-inlet samples in time and space; these are correlated observations, not 52,128 independent experiments.

| Criterion | Verified observation |
|---|---|
| Limiting outage | Both models picked CRAC-1 for C01 and CRAC-3 for B05 and D02 **among the tested conditions**. |
| Worst-case placement order | 2D: D02 < C01 < B05. Nominal CFD: B05 < D02 < C01. Lower is better. |
| Highest inlet, by placement | C01: 34.6450 °C (2D) / 26.371556 °C (CFD); B05: 35.2199 / 25.802882 °C; D02: 33.4162 / 25.997106 °C. |
| D02 vs. current | 1.2288 °C better in the 2D model; 0.37445 °C better in nominal CFD. |
| CFD decision penalty | Choosing 2D winner D02 over nominal CFD winner B05 gives a 0.194224 °C higher CFD worst inlet peak. No discretization or input uncertainty is attached to this difference yet. |
| Pointwise agreement | Pooled MAE 2.237 °C; RMSE 2.984 °C; bias +0.098 °C (2D minus CFD). Small pooled bias masks negative bias under full cooling and positive bias in both outage scenarios. |
| Hottest rack | Exact hottest-slot agreement in 2 of 9 paired cases, both with CRAC-1 off; none with CRAC-3 off. During CRAC-3 outage, CFD puts the hottest inlet at C07 and 2D puts it at A08 for all three placements. |
| Horizon sensitivity, exploratory | Using cumulative envelopes at 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, and 180 s, 2D selects D02 and nominal CFD selects B05 at all six cuts. These are nested windows on the same trajectories, not independent replication. Both decisive CRAC-3 peaks occur at the 180 s boundary. |
| Runtime context | Archived Unity summaries report about 1.1 s wall time for a 32-placement screening run. The nine CFD logs report about 3,213–9,300 s of solver execution time each, excluding the common warm-up and preprocessing. These use different workload and timing boundaries, so they do not establish a controlled speedup ratio. |

Source: the [nominal benchmark archive](../Validation/results/nominal/) and the original local solver reports. The horizon check was computed from the archived one-second traces and should be labeled exploratory.

## Candidate manuscript claim

> We present a matched comparison of 2D thermal screening and 3D CFD for placing a sustained high-load rack under loss of cooling-coil capacity. In the tested hall, the models agreed on which tested outage produced the largest inlet peak for each placement, but not on the nominal ordering of the shortlisted placements. We report outage identification, hotspot location, placement ranking, and the resulting difference in the worst CFD inlet temperature separately from samplewise temperature error.

This is a proposed **empirical contribution**, not a claim to a new optimizer, the first data-center cooling-outage simulation, or a generally predictive result. The two CFD outage cases were chosen from the 2D screening, so agreement on the limiting unit is conditional on that subset. The pooled bias does not show good absolute temperature agreement; positive and negative errors can cancel. The CFD winner is nominal until numerical sensitivity has been assessed.

The three scenario-specific rankings also disagree. The nominal penalty for taking the model's favorite *for each scenario*, instead of the CFD favorite among the same three candidates, is approximately **0.864 °C** (all cooling), **0.053 °C** (CRAC-1), and **0.194 °C** (CRAC-3). These are retrospective diagnostics, not new planned comparisons. They show why a rank reversal and its practical importance should be reported separately.

## What must hold for a strong claim

1. Assess the **signed difference between B05 and D02**, not just the sensitivity of each temperature in isolation. Use a paired time-step test on the original mesh first, and verify the actual time step fell; changing a ceiling above the solver's actual step does not refine it. Then assess mesh refinement at a sufficiently small time step, with the same geometry, matched probes, and equivalent warm-up. A single finer mesh is a sensitivity check, not in itself a formal convergence study. See [NASA Glenn, spatial convergence](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html), [temporal convergence](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/tempconv.html), and [Celik et al., CFD discretization uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.2960953).
2. If the sign persists and the gap is larger than defensible numerical sensitivity, report a decision reversal **for this one benchmark**. If the sign or size does not hold, report the pair as not distinguishable at the tested resolution. Nominal CFD is a computational comparator, not physical ground truth.
3. Simulate CRAC-2 and CRAC-4 before making a claim about *all* individual coil outages. Test additional CFD placements outside the original shortlist before estimating whether screening reliably finds the best of 32. A second geometry, load pattern, or measured case would strengthen a general claim.
4. Package the matched traces, case and probe manifests, and analysis scripts after the results are ready to share. [IEEE Access's reproducibility guidance](https://ieeeaccess.ieee.org/authors/reproducibility/) recommends documenting the workflow and mapping expected outputs back to the figures and tables in the article. The raw solver logs and all intermediate fields need not be versioned to let readers reproduce the comparison from the archived traces.
